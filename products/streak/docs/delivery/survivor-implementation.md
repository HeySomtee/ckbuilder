## Rules frozen before entry

20 October deletion: administrators can choose **Delete competition**, then confirm the named game. Only games with zero entrants can be deleted. PostgreSQL retains a deletion marker, removes the game from reads and rejects later mutations or recreation with the same ID. Admission and deletion lock the same row, so a simultaneous join cannot be lost. The capacity bound applies to 100 active competitions; deleted audit records remain stored. No funds are moved. Games with entrants retain their entries and results.

- Select one to ten full API-Football Premier League rounds available from the cached schedule. Each contains ten fixtures and twenty distinct clubs. Incomplete or overlapping rounds cannot publish. The current schedule cache may not yet contain a complete round; show that honestly and wait for coverage rather than manufacture fixtures.
- All rounds are pinned before publication, including team IDs, fixture IDs, kickoff times and the scheduling policy. Picks close one minute before the earliest kickoff. Results finalize six hours after the last scheduled kickoff. A following round must start after the previous resolution deadline. Published terms cannot be edited, even before the first join in this initial implementation.
- One free entry per authenticated account. Admission is atomic and idempotent. This is not proof of one human. A saved pick can change before the common deadline. Only its owner sees it before that deadline, including when another viewer is an admin.
- Wins advance; draws, losses and missing picks eliminate. A used team cannot be repeated. Unresolved fixtures void at resolution and restore the team. If all actual picks lose or draw, those players are rescued but their teams remain spent. Missing picks never qualify for rescue.
- Changed kickoff times, changed teams and explicit postponed/cancelled/suspended observations latch a fixture as unavailable for this competition. Existing picks void and restore the team. Published deadlines do not move. If the opening slate cannot offer every entrant a legal choice, cancel. If a later slate becomes impossible for any surviving entrant, declare the remaining entrants joint winners.
- Fewer than two opening entrants or everyone missing the pick deadline cancels. The last survivor wins after a final round. At the end of the published schedule, survivors are joint winners. If service recovery misses the next opening deadline, surviving players become joint winners instead of receiving automatic missed-pick eliminations.
- Announced prizes remain distinct from escrow, payment and claimable balances. Joint winners share the announcement equally; a smallest-unit remainder stays unallocated pending the host's separate award arrangement. No transfer or payout proof is claimed.

## State, recovery and source boundaries

`src/modules/survivor` owns rules, projections, PostgreSQL mutations and the provider integration. The API uses the existing session and same-origin JSON mutation policy. Browser responses are validated and checked against the expected viewer. Queries are account-scoped, cleared on identity changes, and refreshed every thirty seconds while visible. Delayed mutations retain their original query key and selection epoch.

The PostgreSQL row lock serializes admissions, picks and progression per competition. Deadline checks use database time after acquiring the lock. A failed mutation rolls back. Idempotent creation identifiers and account admission prevent duplicates. The bounded first release supports up to 100 active competitions, 256 entrants each and ten rounds. Listing reads summaries only; one selected competition supplies entries/history. There is no per-entry API polling or second database framework.

The existing football provider remains trusted. A separate stable observation stream records actual feed merges, including explicit withdrawal of a previous result. Cached reads do not mint new observation timestamps. Up to 64 changes plus an overflow marker are retained per fixture between ingestion cycles. The worker persists the latest eligible observation per pinned round and consumes pre-cutoff history in order. A relevant unconsumed history gap voids the fixture conservatively. Observations after the cutoff cannot replace the last eligible one. A restart never backdates newly fetched observations.

The worker runs in the API's existing bounded tick and still executes when fixture refresh fails. Previously persisted observations survive a restart; absent provider data does not erase them. Explicit newer corrections do. Finalized rounds are immutable and retain their result snapshot, rule version and lifecycle audit events. This is application-enforced free-game logic, not a new trust-minimized Survivor contract.
